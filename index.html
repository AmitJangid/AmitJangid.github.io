About Me

I specialize in the application of mathematical and computational modeling to solve complex biological problems. My work employs stochastic simulations, bifurcation analysis, large-scale data analysis, and network theory to investigate population genetics and regulatory networks.

I obtained my PhD in Computational Biology and Bioinformatics from Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi. Currently, I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, focusing on the evolution of self-incompatibility systems in plants.

Current Research

Evolution of Plant Self-Incompatibility

Studying the S-RNase-based self-incompatibility in the collaborative non-self recognition (CNSR) system. Using theoretical frameworks to understand how promiscuous molecular recognition and multiple distinct partners per protein drive the spontaneous self-organization of populations into distinct mating classes.

Regulatory Networks & Cell Fate

Modeling noise-regulated cell fate decisions and the dynamics of coupled NF-KB oscillators. Previous work includes constructing stress-driven minimal models of the p53 regulatory network to identify pathological states for therapeutic intervention.
